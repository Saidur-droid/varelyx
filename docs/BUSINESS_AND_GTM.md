# Varelyx Business and GTM Plan

## Company thesis

Varelyx is intended to become a real venture-scale software company, not a one-off competition project.

The wedge is **retail continuity under uncertainty**. The long-term category ambition is broader **retail decision infrastructure**.

## Initial ICP

Do not acquire tiny shops one by one.

Primary customers:
- FMCG distributors;
- regional retail chains;
- multi-location grocery networks;
- pharmacy chains;
- general merchandise operators;
- distributors serving hundreds or thousands of retail endpoints.

Typical size:
- 20–500 owned locations; or
- 500–50,000 serviced retail endpoints.

Typical buyers:
- COO;
- Head of Supply Chain;
- Distribution Head;
- Inventory Planning Head;
- Operations Director.

## Core pain

When the operating plan breaks, teams often coordinate through spreadsheets, calls, messaging apps, supplier confirmations, manual scenario analysis, and manager approvals.

The cost is not only forecast error. It is **decision recovery time** and the business exposure that accumulates while the team is uncertain.

Varelyx compresses that fragmented workflow into an evidence-backed decision process.

## Adoption wedge: Shadow Mode

Do not ask customers to replace ERP/POS/WMS/planning software first.

Varelyx should connect read-only and run in parallel.

### Stage 1 — Shadow Audit
- 14–30 days;
- read-only;
- no process migration;
- no forced autonomy.

### Stage 2 — Value Ledger
Record:
- decisions the current process made;
- decisions Varelyx would have changed;
- which differences were material;
- later observed outcome when available;
- source/provenance of every claimed value.

### Stage 3 — Proof of Value
Commercial thesis:

> **We earn the switch before asking for it.**

A strong offer could become:

> If a shadow pilot cannot identify enough verified addressable value to justify the economics, do not sign the annual contract.

This is a GTM philosophy, not a guarantee until real pilots validate the economics.

## Why switching can become compelling

Varelyx should not compete on “our dashboard is nicer.”

The switching/adoption value is:
- faster disruption response;
- explicit uncertainty;
- fewer low-value calls/questions;
- better use of existing stock before emergency procurement;
- protection of working capital;
- verifiable policy compliance;
- auditable decision history;
- ability to coexist with current systems.

## Revenue model hypotheses

### Early design partners
Free or highly discounted short pilot to acquire real workflow/data evidence.

### Growth
Network-based SaaS pricing.

Possible dimensions:
- platform fee;
- number of locations/endpoints;
- transaction/decision volume;
- integrations;
- advanced optimization/evaluation modules.

### Enterprise
Annual platform contracts plus implementation/integration fees where justified.

### Optional later
Performance-linked component against an agreed baseline, only when attribution is strong enough.

Do not hardcode final pricing until willingness-to-pay interviews and pilot economics exist.

## Early ARR ambition

Founder target discussed during planning:
- 25 customers × approximately BDT 24 lakh/year = approximately BDT 6 crore ARR;
- 30 customers × approximately BDT 25 lakh/year = approximately BDT 7.5 crore ARR.

Treat these as aggressive planning scenarios, not forecasts.

A path to larger value requires:
- enterprise retention;
- verified ROI;
- expansion revenue;
- repeatable onboarding;
- strong gross margin;
- data/decision network effects.

## Long-term moat candidates

### Decision Outcome Graph
Event → Evidence → Uncertainty → Decision → Action → Outcome.

### Supplier Reliability Graph
What a supplier promised versus actual fulfilment, especially under stress periods.

### Retail Shock Library
How different network types respond to demand shocks, route disruption, supplier failure, and working-capital pressure.

### Evidence Economics
Which missing facts historically changed decisions most and were worth acquiring.

These are future moat candidates, not day-one claims.

## Bangladesh beachhead

Bangladesh is valuable because fragmented retail/distribution makes coordination and information gaps visible.

But the product must not be Bangladesh-specific.

Positioning:

**Built in Bangladesh. Designed for volatile commerce everywhere.**

The commercial beachhead should be distributors and multi-location operators because one customer can expose Varelyx to hundreds or thousands of retail endpoints.

## Global thesis

Every retailer has plans. Every retailer experiences reality changing faster than the plan.

The structural problem is universal:
- supplier delays;
- demand shocks;
- route disruption;
- incomplete inventory visibility;
- working-capital pressure;
- operational policies;
- human approval bottlenecks.

Varelyx's architecture should therefore support multi-market expansion from the beginning.

## Business validation roadmap

1. 5–10 operator interviews.
2. 2 design-partner candidates.
3. One read-only historical backtest.
4. One Shadow Audit.
5. One Value Ledger with observed outcomes.
6. Convert the strongest pilot into paid annual contract.
7. Standardize onboarding.
8. Repeat within the same vertical before expanding.

## Key business risks

- long enterprise sales cycles;
- poor data quality;
- integration burden;
- inability to attribute ROI;
- incumbent feature convergence;
- procurement resistance;
- customer fear of AI autonomy.

## Risk response

- Shadow Mode reduces migration risk;
- Proof Gate reduces autonomy risk;
- Value Ledger reduces trust/ROI ambiguity;
- bounded wedge reduces integration scope;
- read-only pilot lowers adoption friction;
- deterministic optimization makes economics auditable.

## North-star business metric

Not “number of AI messages.”

Track **verified decision value created or protected per customer** and the time required to reach that value.
